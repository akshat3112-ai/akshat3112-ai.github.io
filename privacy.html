Cutting Chai: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 6 November 2026

Cutting Chai ("the game") is made by Perfect Fifth. This policy explains what information is used when you play.

In short: the game never asks for your name, email or contacts. It sends anonymous game statistics to Google Firebase Analytics and shows ads from Google AdMob. Google collects some device information for these services, as described below.

Game statistics (Google Firebase Analytics)

To learn how the game is played and make it better, the game sends anonymous events to Google Firebase Analytics, for example: a shift started or ended (score, glasses served, perfect pours, flies zapped, burns, how long it lasted, why it ended), a new stage of the game was reached, a tip was closed, a setting was changed, an ad was offered or shown, the Daily Challenge was played, a reminder was set or opened.

These events never contain your name, email address, phone number, contacts or anything you type. Firebase adds information about your device and app (such as model, operating system version, country derived from your IP address, app version, and an app instance ID it creates for this install) and, where your consent allows, uses advertising identifiers. Google keeps event data on its servers for 2 months and user-level data (such as the app instance ID) for 14 months, then deletes it. Google signals (linking this data to Google accounts across devices) is switched off.

How Google uses information from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites Firebase privacy information: https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy

Ads (Google AdMob)

The game shows two kinds of full-screen ads, served by Google AdMob:

  • an optional "Second chance" video you can choose to watch for more time in a shift, and
  • an occasional ad between shifts (never during the Daily Challenge).

There are no banner ads. When the game requests or shows an ad, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects and processes information from your device, such as:

  • your device's advertising ID and other device identifiers,
  • your IP address and the approximate location derived from it,
  • information about your device and the app (model, operating system, language, app version), and how you interact with ads (for example, whether you tapped one),
  • diagnostic data such as crash logs and ad performance.

Google uses this to deliver ads, measure them, limit how often you see them, and prevent fraud. Depending on your choices (see below), the ads may be personalised based on your interests or non-personalised. Cutting Chai does not receive or store this information itself.

Google's privacy policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy

Your choices about ads, statistics and consent

  • UK, European Economic Area and Switzerland: the first time you open the game, Google's consent form asks whether you agree to the use of your data for ads, including personalised ads, and for measurement. Until you answer, the game sends no statistics at all. If you choose "Do not consent", it never sends any: Firebase Analytics stays switched off (no events and no anonymous signals). If you consent, statistics are sent as described above, following the purposes you accepted (Google Consent Mode). You can change your choice at any time in the game: Settings (the gear on the menu) → "Privacy & ads settings"; withdrawing consent switches the statistics off again and erases this install's analytics ID.
  • Everyone: you can reset your advertising ID or opt out of personalised ads in your phone's settings (Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads, depending on the phone).
  • If you play without an internet connection, no ads are requested or shown and no statistics are sent (Firebase may send them later when you are back online).

Reminders (notifications)

If you allow it, the game sets reminders on your own phone: one when the day's chai challenge is ready (16:30 your time, only if you haven't played it yet), and one if you haven't played for two days. They are created by the game on your phone; no server sends them and no information is sent to set them. There is never more than one a day and none between 21:00 and 09:00. Switch them off any time in the game (Settings → Daily reminder) or in your phone's settings. The statistics note when a reminder is set and whether you opened the game from it (not who you are).

What stays on your phone

To work properly, the game keeps a small file on your own phone with:

  • your best score and the furthest glass you've reached,
  • your Daily Challenge score for today and your daily streak,
  • which tips and instructions you've already seen,
  • your settings (music, sound effects, vibration, screen shake, volumes, daily reminder),
  • how many times you've opened the game and played, and when it last asked you to rate it.

This file never leaves your phone. Uninstalling the game, or clearing its storage in your phone's settings, deletes it.

Rating the game

After a good shift the game may show Google Play's own rating card. Anything you write there is handled by Google Play under Google's privacy policy; the game is not told whether or how you rated.

Permissions

  • Vibrate: for short vibrations when you pour, zap a fly and so on. You can switch vibration off in the game.
  • Notifications (Android 13 and newer ask you): for the reminders above. The game asks only after explaining why, and never the first time you play.
  • Run at startup: so your reminders are kept after the phone restarts.
  • Internet and network state: used by Google AdMob and Firebase Analytics.
  • Advertising ID: used by Google AdMob and Firebase for ads and measurement, as described above.

The game asks for no other permissions that need your approval. (Google's SDKs also list a few routine system permissions, such as keeping the device awake while an ad video loads; none of them give access to your contacts, photos, location or files.)

Sharing your score

If you tap "Challenge a friend" or "Share today's result", the game opens WhatsApp (or your browser) with a message about your score already typed. Nothing is sent unless you choose to send it, and whatever you send is handled by that app under its own privacy policy.

Children

The game is made for adults (18 and over) and is not directed at children or teenagers. It does not knowingly collect information from anyone under 18.
